\title{What a Chance Constraint Does Not Promise: Measuring and
Restoring a Demand-Ceiling Guarantee on Metered Load}

% TODO(author): full author list, affiliations, corresponding author.
\author[a]{Swetank Kumar\corref{cor1}}
\ead{dynamicsanvil@gmail.com}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation[a]{organization={Project Samanvay / Aethergrid},
                country={India}}

\begin{abstract}
Indian commercial demand charges are set by the single worst thirty-minute
block in a month, which makes defending a demand ceiling a risk problem rather
than a prediction problem. The standard applied response substitutes a high
forecast quantile into the capacity constraint and calls the result a 95\%
guarantee. This paper measures what that guarantee delivers, on fourteen
held-out windows across an American office and an Indian campus at native
15-minute resolution. Per step the bound behaves as advertised: realised
exceedance is 0.032--0.079 against a nominal 0.05 on every window. Over the
controller's 16-hour planning window the probability of breaching
\emph{somewhere} is 0.39 to 0.82, median 0.57 --- eight to sixteen times the
level the constraint appears to promise --- and it is not a constant of the
formulation, since one meter returns 0.39, 0.64 and 0.42 in three successive
Junes while its per-step rate does not move. Neither textbook substitute
recovers it: the independence calculation overstates realised risk by
1.7$\times$ at the median because load errors are strongly autocorrelated, and
the Boole union bound, the only one of the two that is valid, is 1 on every
window. A Student-$t$ copula fitted on validation data predicts the realised
value to a mean absolute error of 0.065; using it, a scenario MILP restores the
violation level as a settable parameter, tracking the requested $\varepsilon$
at rank correlation 0.957 for \rupee{3{,}339} on a billing month, with its
exactness and its resolution floor reported. The same protocol run over
thirty-seven electricity supplies in eight countries yields two further
results. Distribution-free coverage does \emph{not} degrade with aggregation
level, contrary to a reading this study first made and then unmade by its own
audit: the adaptive conformal layer's step size is stated in the units of the
series, and at system scale it had stopped moving. And forecast skill and
available flexibility are independent deployment axes, only one of which tracks
climate.
\end{abstract}

\begin{highlights}
\item A marginally calibrated demand ceiling breaches somewhere in a 16-hour
      window on 39--82\% of held-out origins, against a nominal 5\%.
\item The gap is measured on fourteen windows in two countries with day-block
      bootstrap intervals, not asserted from one building.
\item Both textbook corrections fail: the independence figure is not a bound,
      the valid bound is vacuous four hours into the horizon.
\item A copula plus a scenario MILP makes the violation level a parameter an
      operator sets, at a measured price and with a stated resolution floor.
\item An aggregation effect the study first reported is retracted: it was the
      adaptive layer's step size stated in the wrong units.
\end{highlights}
